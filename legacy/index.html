        Bet sizing theory

        Game theory starts from one idea: every bet size sets a price. That price tells you how often to defend, how often to bluff, and how often a bluff has to work.

        What each number means

        • Equity to call: your hand must win at least this often to call profitably.
        • Minimum defence frequency (MDF): if you fold more than 1 − MDF of your range, any two cards can bluff you profitably.
        • Bluff success needed: a pure bluff breaks even when villain folds this often.
        • Bluffs in a balanced river bet: the share of your betting range that can be bluffs so villain can't exploit you either way.

        Using it against friends and live fields

        • Balanced play is the baseline. Most home and live low-stakes players under-bluff big bets, so fold more than MDF against them on the river.
        • Players who call too much make bluffing a losing play. Bet bigger for value instead.
        • Online regulars are closer to balanced, so stick nearer to these numbers.

        Weekly routine

        • Before each session: play 20 Practice hands. Aim for 80% best or fine decisions before you sit down.
        • After each tournament: log it here with one hand you weren't sure about. Replay it in Equity or ICM.
        • Before a live event: run two or three bubble spots through ICM with that event's payouts.
        • Once a week: compare ROI live vs online and put more volume where you're winning.

        You need 50–100 events before ROI means much. Tournament results swing hard.
